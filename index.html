David Joseph

I’m a postdoctoral associate at the Technical University of Munich, where I design quantum optimal-control pulses for applications such as magnetic resonance (MR). My work aims to recover sensitivity that would otherwise be lost because of hardware limitations.

More broadly, I develop quantum-control and machine-learning methods to solve problems in the life sciences, physics, and chemistry. One question I hope to address over the course of my career is: How can we map the dynamical properties of biomolecules to their functional properties?

Previously, I was at the Max Planck Institute for Multidisciplinary Sciences in Göttingen, where I first completed my PhD (summa cum laude, 2024) and then worked as a postdoctoral researcher for one year. There, my research focused on developing a robust quantum optimal-control pulse-sequence library for 1.2 GHz spectrometers to overcome instrumental limitations. The resulting publication received the Ernst Award from the Division of Magnetic Resonance of the German Chemical Society (GDCh) and the JMR/JMRO Young Scientist Award at EUROMAR 2024.

Before that, I developed rapid NMR methods for metabolomics at the Indian Institute of Science in Bangalore. I also created Pulsar.jl, an open-source Julia toolbox for designing quantum optimal-control pulses.
