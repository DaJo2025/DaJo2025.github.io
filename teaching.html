Teaching

I have done my fair share of advertising to promote optimal control pulses and helping researchers use them, during my PhD as well as my postdoc. Optimal control has a reputation for being something only a numerics group can do. Most of my teaching effort goes into demonstrating that this is no longer true.

Using optimal control pulses on your own spectrometer

The most common question I get after a talk is some version of “this is nice, but could I actually run it here?” The answer is usually yes, and it does not require becoming an optimal control researcher. Three practical routes, in increasing order of effort:

  • Use a published pulse directly. The pulse shapes from the 1.2 GHz work are distributed with the papers. For many experiments this is a drop-in replacement for the shaped pulse you are already using.
  • Re-optimize for your hardware. Probe limits and B1 profiles differ between instruments. Pulsar.jl lets you re-run the optimization with your own constraints — the documentation has a quickstart that goes end to end.
  • Design for an experiment that does not exist yet. This is the interesting case, and the one I am always happy to talk through. Get in touch.
